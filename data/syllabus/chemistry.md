# Chemistry syllabus map (Singapore)

Official MOE/SEAB **learning-outcome maps** for RAG. Not encyclopedic chemistry notes.

Chemistry is not a separate subject until upper secondary. Primary and lower-secondary outcomes here are the chemical-science topics from MOE Science syllabuses. Biology-only topics are omitted. Combined Science 5086 chemistry is kept alongside 6092.

Sources: MOE Primary Science Syllabus 2023; MOE 2021 G2/G3 and G1 Lower Secondary Science (updated April 2024); SEAB GCE O-Level 2026 Chemistry 6092 and Combined Science (Physics, Chemistry) 5086; SEAB GCE A-Level 2026 H1 Chemistry 8873 and H2 Chemistry 9476 (revised; 9729 is last examined in 2026).

## Primary Science — Diversity of materials (MOE Primary Science Syllabus 2023)

P3 chemical-science topic (also mapped in physics for properties of matter):

- Relate wood, metal, ceramic, rubber, glass, plastic and fabric to physical properties.
- Compare strength, flexibility, ability to float or sink, waterproof, and transparency.
- Density is not required. Terms transparent/translucent/opaque are not required.
- Safety: never taste chemicals; report spills; follow teacher instructions (classroom practice, not a numbered LO).

## Primary Science — Matter, melting, boiling and the water cycle (MOE Primary Science Syllabus 2023)

Cycles in matter and water — Matter (P4):

- Matter is anything that has mass and occupies space.
- Differentiate solid, liquid and gas in terms of shape and volume.
- Measure mass and volume using appropriate apparatus.

Cycles in matter and water — Water (P5 Standard; Foundation is a reduced list):

- Water can exist in three interchangeable states.
- Melting (solid to liquid), freezing (liquid to solid), boiling/evaporation (liquid to gas), condensation (gas to liquid).
- Melting point of ice (freezing point of water) and boiling point of water.
- Roles of evaporation and condensation in the water cycle; importance of the water cycle.
- Ice melts at 0 °C; water freezes at 0 °C; water boils at 100 °C; steam condenses to water.
- Rate of evaporation affected by wind, temperature and exposed surface area.
- Importance of water to life processes; impact of water pollution; water as a limited resource.

Atomic structure, bonding, the mole and electrolysis are not Primary outcomes.

## Secondary Lower-secondary Science — Elements, compounds, mixtures and particles (MOE 2021 G2/G3 Lower Secondary Science, updated Apr 2024)

Exploring diversity of matter by its physical properties:

- Describe physical properties that can be observed or measured (electrical and thermal conductivity, melting/boiling point; strength, hardness, flexibility and density — some optional for G2).
- Density = mass/volume; predict sink or float by comparing densities; measure length, mass and volume; volume displacement for irregular objects (optional for G2).

Exploring diversity of matter by its chemical composition:

- Elements are the basic building blocks; metals and non-metals in the Periodic Table.
- Compounds consist of two or more chemically combined elements and have different characteristics from those elements.
- Mixtures are not chemically combined and display characteristics of their constituents.
- Solute, solvent and solution; solutions and suspensions are mixtures.
- Distinguish elements, compounds and mixtures; factors affecting rate of dissolving (solubility optional for G2).

Exploring diversity of matter using separation techniques: classify and choose methods such as filtration, evaporation, distillation and paper chromatography at lower-secondary depth (full 6092 apparatus list waits for O-Level).

Model of matter — particulate nature of matter: particles in solids, liquids and gases; arrangement and motion; diffusion.

Model of matter — atoms and molecules:

- An atom is electrically neutral with a positive nucleus (protons and neutrons) and electrons moving around the nucleus.
- Atoms of an element have a unique number of protons.
- A molecule is two or more atoms chemically combined.
- State numbers and types of atoms from a given formula (writing formulae and giant molecular compounds are not required).

G1 Lower Secondary Science covers these chemical-science themes at reduced depth.

## Secondary Lower-secondary Science — Chemical changes (MOE 2021 G2/G3 Lower Secondary Science, updated Apr 2024)

- A chemical change forms new substance(s).
- Use word equations (chemical equations are not required).
- Atoms are rearranged, not created or destroyed; mass is conserved.
- Types of chemical change: combustion, thermal decomposition, oxidation (e.g. rusting and cellular respiration) and neutralisation.
- Investigate acids and alkalis; acids and metals; acids and carbonates (some optional for G2).
- Effect of acidic, alkaline and neutral solutions on litmus, Universal Indicator and plant indicators.
- Changes on mixing, heating, exposure to light, interacting with oxygen; using an electric current (e.g. electroplating) is optional for G2.

O-Level electrolysis, the mole, and organic homologous series are not lower-secondary required outcomes.

## O-Level Chemistry 6092 — Experimental chemistry and particulate nature of matter (SEAB 2026)

Section I Matter — structures and properties.

Experimental chemistry:

- Apparatus for time, temperature, mass and volume including burettes, pipettes, measuring cylinders and gas syringes.
- Separation: suitable solvent, filtration, crystallisation or evaporation; sublimation; distillation and fractional distillation; separating funnel; paper chromatography including R_f and locating agents.
- Melting point and boiling point as evidence of identity and purity (e.g. foodstuffs and drugs).
- Drying agents limited to calcium oxide, concentrated sulfuric acid and fused calcium chloride.

Particulate nature of matter:

- Kinetic particle theory for solid, liquid and gas and their interconversion; diffusion (Brownian motion is not required); effect of molecular mass and temperature on rate of diffusion.
- Atomic structure: relative charges and masses of proton, neutron and electron; nucleus and electron shells (s, p, d, f classification is not required); proton number, nucleon number, nuclide notation, isotopes.

## O-Level Chemistry 6092 — Bonding, structure and chemical calculations (SEAB 2026)

Chemical bonding and structure: ionic, covalent and metallic bonding; structure linked to physical properties (melting/boiling point, electrical conductivity). Hybridisation and VSEPR at H2 depth are not O-Level.

Chemical calculations:

- Symbols and formulae; ionic formulae from charges; chemical equations with state symbols including ionic equations.
- The mole; Avogadro constant; empirical and molecular formulae; reacting masses; volumetric analysis (titration calculations).

## O-Level Chemistry 6092 — Acids, bases, salts, qualitative analysis, redox and electrolysis (SEAB 2026)

Acid-base chemistry: acids, bases and salts; pH; oxides; preparation of salts.

Qualitative analysis: identification of ions and gases as specified in the 6092 notes (tests for common cations, anions and gases).

Redox chemistry:

- Oxidation and reduction in terms of oxygen/hydrogen, electron transfer and oxidation state.
- Aqueous potassium iodide and acidified potassium manganate(VII) as tests for oxidising and reducing agents.
- Electrolysis: conduction through a molten or aqueous ionic compound with chemical change at the electrodes; evidence for mobile ions; electrolysis of molten sodium chloride; selective discharge (reactivity series for cations; halides, hydroxide and sulfate for anions; concentration effects for aqueous NaCl); ionic equations at electrodes; purification of copper by electrolysis of aqueous copper(II) sulfate with copper electrodes; electroplating; simple cells linked to the reactivity series; hydrogen fuel cell (construction details not required).

## O-Level Chemistry 6092 — Periodic table, energetics, rates, organic chemistry and air quality (SEAB 2026)

Patterns in the Periodic Table: arrangement by increasing proton number; Group I, Group VII, noble gases; transition elements at O-Level depth; reactivity series of metals.

Chemical energetics: energy changes from chemicals; exothermic and endothermic reactions at O-Level depth (Gibbs free energy is A-Level).

Rate of reactions: factors affecting speed of reaction (concentration, temperature, particle size, catalyst) at O-Level depth.

Organic chemistry:

- Fuels: natural gas (mainly methane) and crude oil as non-renewable; fractional distillation of crude oil; bioethanol as a renewable alternative.
- Hydrocarbons: homologous series; alkanes CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ (C1–C4); isomerism; alkanes generally unreactive except combustion and chlorine substitution; alkenes CₙH₂ₙ; cracking; aqueous bromine test; addition of bromine, steam and hydrogen; margarine by hydrogenating unsaturated oils.
- Alcohols (–OH), carboxylic acids (–CO₂H) and esters: methanol to butanol; combustion and oxidation of alcohols to carboxylic acids; ethanol by hydration of ethene and by fermentation; carboxylic acids as weak acids; ethanoic acid by oxidation of ethanol; esterification (e.g. ethyl ethanoate).
- Polymers: monomers; addition polymerisation of ethene to poly(ethene); nylon (polyamide) and Terylene (polyester) as condensation polymers; uses of man-made fibres.

Maintaining air quality: atmosphere and environment; pollutants and mitigation as specified in 6092 Topic 12.

## O-Level Combined Science 5086 — Chemistry component (SEAB 2026)

Science (Physics, Chemistry) 5086 Chemistry uses the same section headings as 6092: experimental chemistry; particulate nature of matter; chemical bonding and structure; chemical calculations; acid-base chemistry; qualitative analysis; redox chemistry (including electrolysis); patterns in the Periodic Table; chemical energetics; rate of reactions; organic chemistry; maintaining air quality.

Keep Combined Science 5086 with Chemistry where a student takes 5086 rather than 6092. Depth is reduced relative to 6092 (fewer organic and qualitative-analysis details). Hybridisation, VSEPR, chemical equilibria, and Gibbs free energy are not 5086 outcomes.

## A-Level H1 Chemistry 8873 — Core physical and inorganic chemistry (SEAB 2026)

Revised H1 (2026). Topic families:

- Atomic structure (including s and p orbitals at H1 depth; full d-orbital transition chemistry is H2).
- Chemical bonding (ionic, covalent, metallic, intermolecular forces; hybridisation and VSEPR at H2 depth are beyond typical H1 emphasis).
- Theories of acids and bases.
- The Periodic Table.
- The mole concept and stoichiometry.
- Chemical energetics: thermochemistry (not full Gibbs thermodynamics).
- Reaction kinetics.
- Chemical equilibria.
- Polymers and organic chemistry (H1 organic is slimmer than H2 Section 11).

Electrochemistry of cells and electrolysis at H2 depth, and transition-element chemistry, are H2 9476 extension topics, not this H1 list.

## A-Level H2 Chemistry 9476 — Atomic structure, bonding, gases, acids and periodicity (SEAB 2026)

Revised H2 (prefer 9476 over 9729). Core ideas: matter; structure and properties; transformation.

- Atomic structure: protons, neutrons, electrons; isotopes; s, p and d orbitals for n = 1–3 plus 4s and 4p; shapes of s, p and d orbitals; electronic configuration; successive ionisation energies.
- Chemical bonding: ionic, metallic, covalent and dative bonding; shapes and bond angles; bond polarity; intermolecular forces including hydrogen bonding; bond energy and bond length; lattice structure; physical properties.
- Gaseous state: kinetic particle theory; pV = nRT for an ideal gas.
- Theories of acids and bases.
- The Periodic Table: trends in melting point, ionisation energy, atomic and ionic radius, and chemical reactivity.

## A-Level H2 Chemistry 9476 — Stoichiometry, energetics, kinetics, equilibria and aqueous chemistry (SEAB 2026)

- The mole concept and stoichiometry.
- Chemical energetics: thermochemistry and thermodynamics including Gibbs free energy (feasibility).
- Reaction kinetics.
- Chemical equilibria.
- Chemistry of aqueous solutions: ionic equilibria; solubility product; common ion effect; complex ions (e.g. halide ions with aqueous silver ions then aqueous ammonia).

## A-Level H2 Chemistry 9476 — Organic chemistry, electrochemistry and transition elements (SEAB 2026)

Organic chemistry (Section 11): empirical, molecular, structural, displayed and skeletal formulae; functional groups; σ and π bonds; isomerism; hydrocarbons; halogenoalkanes; hydroxy compounds; carbonyl compounds; carboxylic acids and derivatives; nitrogen compounds; electrophilic and nucleophilic mechanisms as specified in 9476. Reagents, essential conditions and major products are required; detailed temperatures, pressures and full practical procedures are not.

Electrochemistry: redox as electron transfer and oxidation-number change; standard electrode potentials E⦵ and standard cell potentials; standard hydrogen electrode; batteries and fuel cells; electrolysis, Faraday constant, Avogadro constant, and industrial uses of electrolysis.

Transition elements: an introduction to the chemistry of transition elements (H2 extension).
